Midterms 2026: A Scenario Tree for the US Elections

The midterm elections on 3 November — and what their outcome means all the way to the end of Trump’s term in January 2029.

Wondering how it will turn out?

Who will win the US House and Senate? And which realistic sub-scenarios follow?

On Tuesday, 3 November 2026, Americans elect the entire House of Representatives and a third of the Senate. For strategic planning, the interesting question is not who wins by how much. It is what each combination of results does to the rest of Donald Trump’s term: to the Republican Party, to American institutions, to markets — and, by extension, to European business, which is tied to all of it through tariffs, exchange rates and security.

This document is a working scenario tree: five main election outcomes, 27 sub-scenarios beneath them, each with an estimated conditional probability, an extrapolation to the end of the term in January 2029, and a verdict on which way the balance of power inside the Republican Party tips.

How to read the model in 60 seconds

Four reading rules

Subjective estimates, not predictions

The probabilities are the author’s calibrated personal estimates as of 22 September 2026, not the output of a poll or a statistical model. The goal is not to be right but to be revisable: this is a rolling model, and its first major test is the election result itself.

Probabilities are conditional

The weights of the main scenarios S1–S5 are rough ranges. Sub-scenario weights apply within their parent scenario and sum to roughly 100% there. So “S1b: 25%” does not mean 25% overall; it means 25% given that outcome S1 has occurred.

Will Trump keep the loyalty of his administration and the Republican Party?

That is the model’s central question, and we approach it through the lens of a cascade. Studying the revolutions of 1989, economist Timur Kuran described preference falsification: people publicly support a regime they privately do not believe in, because defection is punished. When the first defectors get away unscathed, the rest discover they are not alone — and support does not erode gradually, it collapses in a leap. Here we apply that framework to Republican elites. The question is not “who wins the election” but “when and how do the elites stop being afraid.” The trigger is usually a common-knowledge event: a moment when everyone knows that everyone knows.

Every sub-scenario has a verdict

BREAKCONSOLIDATIONMIXED

Break: the elites break away from Trump. Consolidation: the elites close ranks. Mixed: creeping, conditional or contradictory trajectories. Calendar anchors: new Congress in January 2027, primaries from January 2028, presidential election in November 2028, end of term on 20 January 2029.

Map of the tree

Five main outcomes and their rough weights

  • S1Democrats win the House, Republicans hold the Senate~40–50%

    Historically the most common outcome: the president’s party loses the House. Divided government and Democratic investigative dominance, but no way to remove Trump from office.

  • S2Democrats win both chambers~20–30%

    Full investigative and legislative dominance; the fastest erosion dynamics.

  • S3Republicans hold both chambers~10–20%

    The outcome that would legitimize the punishment of defectors — and refute the cascade hypothesis.

  • S4Democratic landslide~5%

    A crushing victory. A textbook cascade, playing out live.

  • S5Contested results~5–10%

    Not a standalone outcome but a layer that can sit on top of S1, S2 or S4.

Scenario S1 · weight ~40–50%

Democrats take the House, Republicans hold the Senate

S1a

“2019 Redux”

27%

Trump rhetorically reframes the loss of the House — declaring the retained Senate a victory, exactly as in 2018. The House investigates but stops short of impeachment; legislative stalemate sets in, and governing moves to executive orders.

Extrapolation to January 2029

Slow erosion — the courts gradually restore the White House access stripped from media outlets (~2027); Vice President J. D. Vance stays loyal until summer 2027, then cautiously starts building “his own vision.” Trump’s last lever is his endorsements in the 2028 primaries — and that currency is losing value. By January 2029, a “managed twilight”: Trump as a kingmaker with a falling stock price.

MIXED — a creeping break with no triggering event

S1b

“The Investigation Mill”

25%

Hakeem Jeffries becomes Speaker, and 2027 is dominated by subpoenas and public hearings; the House forces a vote on releasing the Epstein files, which are sensitive for Trump’s own base.

Extrapolation to January 2029

True to form, Trump steps up the punishment of defectors — but every lost court battle is public, and a string of defeats works as a chain of common-knowledge events. The first punished defector weathers the penalty around spring 2027 and defection thresholds fall; major donors shift to the 2028 race from autumn 2027; Fox News changes its tone only behind its audience, not ahead of it.

BREAK — medium intensity, 2027–28

S1c

“Impeachment #3”

12%

Triggered by defiance of court rulings or an escalation of the Iran conflict. The House impeaches Trump — for the third time in his career — but the Senate acquits: conviction requires 67 of 100 senators, a threshold that is out of reach.

Extrapolation to January 2029

A paradoxical effect. The base rallies (impeachment becomes fuel for a grievance narrative heading into 2028), while the elites privately conclude that the president is legally besieged; Vance gains ground as “continuity without chaos.” The hardest environment in the entire tree for reading indicators.

MIXED

S1d

“Executive Maximalism”

20%

Governing by decree, impoundment and emergency powers. The institutional apparatus around OMB turns this into the default mode of governing rather than an escape hatch; the Supreme Court becomes the arbiter.

Extrapolation to January 2029

A fork: (i) the Supreme Court blocks two or three major moves → elite thresholds fall fast, Republican governors distance themselves, a break by the end of 2027; (ii) the Court defers to the executive → consolidation despite the electoral defeat, and the conflict shifts to the states and the financial markets.

BREAK if the Court blocks / CONSOLIDATION if it defers

S1e

“Narrow Loss and Seat Disputes”

10%

Margins of one to three seats, contested results, a delayed Speaker vote — a bridge to the dynamics of scenario S5.

Extrapolation to January 2029

Months of uncertainty, markets penalize it, both parties radicalize their narratives; however the dispute ends, the House’s legitimacy stays dented for the whole period.

MIXED with tail risk

S1f

“Deal-Making Pivot”

8%

Trump surprises with transactional cooperation: legislative concessions in exchange for quiet on the investigative front. Unlikely given his personality — but the Trump family, with its business interests and concern for his legacy, is the strongest internal lobby for a deal.

Extrapolation to January 2029

De-escalation on the media front (a quiet restoration of press credentials), “boredom as a strategy”; the base is confused, and Ron DeSantis attacks from the right.

MIXED — a break by the soft road

Scenario S2 · weight ~20–30%

Democrats take both chambers

S2a

“Subpoena Avalanche and the Bunker”

25%

Both chambers investigate and the Senate blocks nominations — the government slides into rule by “acting” secretaries, and a cabinet exodus follows during 2027.

Extrapolation to January 2029

The cost of elite conformity jumps — Kuran’s tipping point. Donors defect in the first half of 2027, the Murdoch media empire switches sides, the first senators speak openly; Trump is on the defensive for the entire period, and the 2028 primaries proceed without his veto.

BREAK by mid-2027, the base holds

S2b

“Impeachment with Real Teeth”

20%

Conviction remains unlikely, but after a partial break 10–15 Republican votes in the Senate become conceivable — impeachment stops being a one-off event and becomes a permanent pressure tool.

Extrapolation to January 2029

Trump’s energy goes into defense rather than his agenda; the executive is paralyzed; Vance effectively takes over the day-to-day running of government, and the succession question opens early.

BREAK

S2c

“Forced Coexistence”

20%

Trump makes concessions in exchange for protection of his legacy and family interests; a quiet non-aggression pact with the Democratic leadership takes shape.

Extrapolation to January 2029

The calmest trajectory — media bans are quietly lifted, the punishments stop, the elites gain independence without drama; by January 2029, a normalized lame duck.

BREAK without a cascade event — a slow deflation

S2d

“Constitutional Crisis”

25%

Defiance of subpoenas and court orders, contempt proceedings — and an open question of who actually enforces rulings when the executive refuses to comply.

Extrapolation to January 2029

Markets punish hard (bonds above all), the institutionalist wing of the Republicans around the Senate leader splits off to defend the Senate as an institution, and governors of both parties coordinate. The highest systemic stress outside scenario S5; the outcome depends on the Supreme Court and on the civil service’s willingness to carry out orders.

BREAK via chaos, with a risk of lasting damage to the rules of the game

S2e

“An Early Succession War”

10%

DeSantis and other governors come out openly as early as winter 2026/27; Trump loses the kingmaker role before he has time to punish anyone.

Extrapolation to January 2029

Vance’s dilemma peaks around autumn 2027 — loyalty turns into a liability; the 2028 primaries become a referendum on Trumpism without Trump.

BREAK via succession

Scenario S3 · weight ~10–20%

Republicans hold both chambers

S3a

“Narrow Hold = Vindication”

40%

The election result retroactively blesses the punishment of defectors: escalation on the media front, retribution against those who distanced themselves, the courts as the only brake.

Extrapolation to January 2029

The elites stay fully conformist at least until the 2028 primaries, where quiet positioning only then begins. Consolidation here means more than additional personal power — it means an accelerated rebuild of institutions along the budget and personnel axis. It is also a formal falsification point: if it materializes, the cascade hypothesis must be revised, not rescued.

CONSOLIDATION — hypothesis must be revised

S3b

“Comfortable Hold”

15%

Full consolidation; the rebuild of institutions accelerates, and testing constitutional limits (including talk of a third term) becomes a permanent pressure tool.

Extrapolation to January 2029

A demoralized opposition; the media landscape restructures (capitulations, sales).

CONSOLIDATION, deep

S3c

“Hold with Surviving Defectors”

25%

The Republicans hold — but candidates who won by distancing themselves from Trump survive unscathed.

Extrapolation to January 2029

Vindication on the surface; underneath, a quiet precedent that “distancing doesn’t kill” — defection thresholds fall even in victory. The trickiest sub-scenario to read: consolidation headlines over eroding foundations; the break shifts to the 2028 primaries.

MIXED — the most diagnostically valuable sub-scenario in the entire tree

S3d

“Rally Win”

20%

A win credited to a last-minute foreign-policy success — for example, ending the Iran conflict.

Extrapolation to January 2029

Trump dominates the 2027 agenda from a position of victory — but the economy still decides. If inflation persists, the cascade is postponed rather than cancelled; a second wave of erosion comes around 2028, amplified by disappointment over “a war won with no dividend.”

CONSOLIDATION short-term, MIXED through the end of the term

Scenario S4 · weight ~5%

Democratic landslide

S4a

“Instant Lame Duck”

40%

Elite defections within weeks of the election — a textbook cascade, playing out live.

Extrapolation to January 2029

DeSantis announces his candidacy in early 2027, and Vance is forced to set out his own vision by the summer; the courts sweep away the media bans, which are already being ignored in practice. At the 2028 nominating convention, Trump is more a tolerated symbol than the head of the party.

BREAK, rapid

S4b

“Scorched Earth”

30%

Trump accuses his own party of betrayal — the 2018 pattern in its extreme form; threats of a third-party run or of sabotaging Republican candidates in 2028.

Extrapolation to January 2029

A party torn between its base and its elites; the 2028 primaries as a civil war; a possible lasting split of the American right, with effects beyond the horizon of this term. More chaotic for the system than S4a; for Trump personally, the last real lever.

BREAK among the elites + CONSOLIDATION of the remaining base — a split

S4c

“Health / Personal Wildcard”

10%

Stepping down for personal reasons — Trump turned eighty in June 2026.

Extrapolation to January 2029

Vance effectively takes over the executive; a managed transition, with the family as custodian of the brand; the cascade plays out quietly and with no one to blame.

BREAK without conflict

S4d

“Institutional Pact”

20%

The Republican Senate minority works with Democrats on safeguards: limits on the president’s war powers, statutory protection for inspectors general, press access guaranteed by law.

Extrapolation to January 2029

A rare window of bipartisan cooperation in 2027; Trump is legislatively isolated, vetoing bills and being overridden on consensus legislation; the precedent of constraining the executive outlives his term.

BREAK, institutionalized — the most durable variant

Scenario S5 · weight ~5–10%, conditional on a loss

Contested results

A layer over S1, S2 or S4 — not a standalone election outcome.

S5a

“Rhetorical”

45%

Claims of fraud, de facto acceptance of the result.

Extrapolation to January 2029

A stolen-election narrative as mobilizing fuel for 2028; systemically manageable, it merges with the S1/S2 trajectories — only at a higher temperature.

As in the underlying scenario, with higher polarization

S5b

“Certification Battles”

25%

Pressure on secretaries of state and the other officials who certify the results.

Extrapolation to January 2029

The 2020 pattern — officials hold, courts rule against; every defeat is a public common-knowledge event, so the pressure campaign paradoxically accelerates the cascade. Governors, Republicans included, gain stature as defenders of the process heading into 2028.

BREAK accelerated by failed repression

S5c

“Executive Intervention”

15%

Executive orders on “election integrity,” Justice Department interventions, talk of federalizing the National Guard.

Extrapolation to January 2029

The Supreme Court is pivotal; the Pentagon enters as an actor in its own right — military doctrine requires refusing an unlawful order — making this the sharpest test of civil–military relations, amplified by the ongoing conflict with Iran. A fork: if the institutions hold, a hard break with legal aftershocks through 2029; if they give way, we leave the space this model is able to describe.

BREAK at a high price / catastrophic tail

S5d

“Violence and Unrest”

10%

Decentralized violence around the certification of results.

Extrapolation to January 2029

The elite reaction decides — the pattern after 6 January 2021 (brief condemnation, then back in line) versus lasting defection this time; it depends on casualties and images. Base fatigue over the Epstein affair may dampen the willingness to mobilize compared with 2021.

Undecided — most sensitive to the first week of reactions

S5e

“Successful Reversal of the Result”

5%

Would require coordination among states, courts and Congress that does not exist.

Extrapolation to January 2029

Realistically, prolonged uncertainty into December 2026 rather than an actual reversal; lasting damage to trust in elections for a decade; markets punish hard.

A systemic event outside the model’s frame

S5f

“Election-Day Gray Zone”

no weight

Intimidation by “threat without an order” — walked-back statements, street incidents — is already partly a reality; the extreme form, visible security operations during early voting, remains unlikely (a signed order would mean personal criminal liability).

Extrapolation to January 2029

The effect lands before the result does — in close districts it marginally shifts the distribution between S1 and S3, while handing Democrats both mobilizing and legal ammunition. Net effect indeterminate.

A modifier of the distribution, not a standalone end state

Across the tree

Seven wild cards

Any one of them can rewrite the weights of any sub-scenario.

  • Iran

    An end to the conflict = a rally effect (a shift toward S3d); escalation and casualties = an accelerant of erosion; fuel prices as a direct electoral channel.

  • The economy

    Stagflationary inertia underlies every form of erosion; relief — a tariff deal, say — is the only realistic way to turn the mood around.

  • The Epstein files

    The only known lever on the base (not on the elites); a forced release of the files changes the parameters of every scenario.

  • Health

    A quiet tail across all scenarios (age 80); see S4c.

  • The Supreme Court

    Arbiter in S1d, S2d and S5c; its own credibility is also on the line.

  • Markets and the Fed

    The bond market as an unelected disciplinarian; high rates shrink the room to “buy voters” through the budget.

  • Murdoch media

    The moment Fox News changes its tone can serve as a leading indicator for every break trajectory.

And one battle beneath the surface

Across all the post-defeat branches runs an internal struggle between two factions in Trump’s orbit: the anti-establishment camp (the ideological wing around the budget and personnel axis) versus the family (the transactional wing protecting business and legacy). If the anti-establishment camp wins, the tree tilts toward the escalatory paths (S2d); if the family wins, toward the transactional ones (S2c, S1f). What to watch: pre-emptive pardons for the family circle, reinforcement of the ideological axis with personnel after a defeat, and the behavior of family business assets around shocks. A chapter of its own is the ongoing purge of the military command: in the short term it favors the transactional side, in the long term the anti-establishment camp — so military tail risks grow over time and are higher for the 2028 election than for these midterms.

Once the actor modules are factored in, weight within the Break category shifts from chaotic forms (S2d) to transactional ones (S2c, S1f). The most likely outcome makes no headlines

The most probable path is not a dramatic cascade but a creeping break with no triggering event (the S1a type). Monitoring has to be able to read the erosion nobody writes about.

S3c is the most valuable

It separates the election result from the actual balance of power inside the party.

A falsification commitment

If S3a or S3b materializes, the honest move is to formally revise the cascade hypothesis, not to rescue it. A model that cannot be refuted is not a model.

Open branches

What the model deliberately leaves out

The biggest gap is the succession question. So far only the trajectory of Vice President J. D. Vance has been worked out — yet Secretary of State Marco Rubio is a real alternative too, and in a fringe scenario even an attempt at a third Trump candidacy cannot be ruled out; with no real constitutional path, it works more as a permanent bargaining chip than as a viable option. The succession branches will be developed on a rolling basis — the model is designed to roll forward, with a revision after each test point. The next one is 3 November.

Inspiration

Try building your own scenario tree

This is not a how-to guide — a full model of this kind takes time, a broad input base and the discipline of regular revisions. But the skeleton of the process is transferable, and even a simplified version — for entering a new market, company succession or industry regulation — will organize your thinking better than a single number in a spreadsheet.

  1. Question and horizon

    Not “what will happen,” but “what will each resolution do to our decision X.” Add calendar anchors — the dates when the uncertainty breaks.

  2. Main branches

    3–6 mutually exclusive outcomes of the key uncertainty, with rough weights. More branches mean less thinking per branch.

  3. Sub-scenarios with conditional weights

    Within each branch, play out variants that sum to 100%. This is where the value is created: it forces you to say out loud what distinguishes the variants.

  4. Verdicts and indicators

    Choose 2–4 categories of resolution that fit your own question, and give each branch leading indicators — what you will see before it makes the news.

  5. Falsification commitment and revision rhythm

    Write down in advance which outcome would refute your hypothesis, and the date of the next revision. A rolling model with a revision trail is worth more than a precise one-off forecast.

The role of AI in this document. The model was built in an “AI as sparring partner, not oracle” mode: generating and stress-testing sub-scenarios, extrapolating to the end of the term, checking the consistency of conditional weights, and feeding new information into the existing tree. The probability estimates, the choice of framework and the falsification commitments are human — which is exactly why they can be defended, revised and, one day, evaluated.
